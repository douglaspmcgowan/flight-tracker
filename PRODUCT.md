# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Flight Finder serves a self-hosting traveler who wants to evaluate a specific trip without reopening several airline, cash-fare, and award-search sites. The primary user is comfortable with airport codes, loyalty programs, and fare tradeoffs. Their first job is a quick answer; their second job is inspecting the evidence behind it.

## Product Purpose

Flight Finder records cash fares and award availability over time so a traveler can decide when and how to book. Success means creating a tracked route quickly, understanding the current best cash and points options, seeing how fresh each observation is, and setting a useful alert without leaving the route workspace.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exacting, traveled. The product borrows the confidence of a well-run departure hall and the visual memory of mid-century airline timetables. It should make complicated fare evidence feel legible and trustworthy.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards assembled from interchangeable cards.
- Gamified points tools that hide taxes, freshness, or the booking program behind a deal score.
- Dense expert tools that expose every filter before the user has chosen a route.
- Decorative “AI travel” styling, purple gradients, amber-primary palettes, and glass panels.

## Design Principles

- Lead with the next decision. Simple mode answers whether a useful option exists and what action follows.
- Let evidence expand. Analyst mode reveals the comparison table, valuation assumptions, filters, and history.
- Show provenance at the point of use. Every result identifies its source, observation time, and cached-data status.
- Keep cash and points comparable while preserving their different units. Taxes and miles remain visible beside any derived cents-per-point value.
- Preserve user control. Search, refresh, alert creation, mode switching, and comparison assumptions remain reversible and explicit.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

ASSUMED: WCAG 2.2 AA is the baseline. All controls need keyboard access, visible focus, useful labels, and state announcements. Color never carries availability or value alone. Motion remains brief, communicates state, and respects reduced-motion preferences. Dense tables must retain a usable mobile reading order.
